# Supported features

Statuses: **Supported** · **Partial** · **Planned** · **Not planned** · **Blocked**

"Tests" names the test class or golden that covers the row. An unsupported construct always produces a
structured diagnostic; nothing is silently ignored (PROJECT_BRIEF.md 3.3, 14).

**Read the Marks table carefully.** A row says "Supported" only when the engine can *produce* that
construct from an input specification. Where a scene node type exists but nothing encodes data into it
yet, the row says **Renderable** — the geometry draws and hit-tests correctly, but there is no mark
encoder, so a Vega specification cannot ask for it. Six of the twelve mark types are now Supported; see
the scope note in STATUS.md for how much of upstream Vega remains.

## Vega-Lite

`vega-lite` is a compiler, not a second engine: it turns a Vega-Lite specification into a Vega one in
the same value model the runtime parses, so from that point a Vega-Lite chart takes exactly the path a
Vega chart does. The rules are ported from upstream's TypeScript sources rather than inferred from its
documentation, and `scripts/vega-lite-oracle.sh` checks the result against upstream twice over: the
Vega it emits, property by property, and the scene that Vega then draws, mark by mark.

| Polar positions (`theta`, `radius`) | **Supported** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` (`pie`, `donut`) | An arc is a rectangle in polar coordinates, which is how upstream compiles one too: the same rect rules serve both, and `theta`/`theta2`/`radius`/`radius2` are written as Vega's `startAngle`/`endAngle`/`outerRadius`/`innerRadius`. An arc stacks by default, in polar coordinates, and a chart with no Cartesian position is styled `view` rather than `cell` — it has no plotting area to draw a border around | — |
| Nested offsets (`xOffset`, `yOffset`) | **Supported** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` (`grouped-bar`, `offset-variants`, `offset-without-position`) | An offset with **no position** beside it has no band to sit inside and spans the whole plot from the middle, `[-width/2, width/2]`; a *continuous* position bucketed by a time unit does have one, a bucket wide, measured through the scale and inset by half the nested padding at each end. Otherwise: The channel that puts bars side by side within a band. Four things follow from it and all four are upstream's: the offset is a band scale of its own, the outer band takes the wider `bandWithNestedOffset` paddings, the bar's width comes from the *inner* scale, and the outer step becomes `20 * bandspace(domain(offset), 0, 0) / (1 - paddingInner)` — as many marks as the group holds, divided by what the padding takes | — |
| Drawing order (`order`) | **Supported** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` (`connected-scatter`) | `getSort`: an `order` channel names the drawing order outright and the mark is sorted by that column rather than by its position — a line running through the years rather than left to right, which is the whole of what a connected scatter plot is. A **stacked** mark is the exception, where `order` says how the segments stack | — |
| A domain written as dates | **Supported** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` (`dated-domain`) | A `DateTime` in a scale's domain becomes the expression that builds the instant, wrapped in `{data: …}` so Vega reads it as one *datum* of the domain rather than a list to be spread | — |

| Scale inference | **Supported** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest`, (`one-field-two-channels`) | A stated `scheme` is the scale's **range** — `parseScheme` — and not a property beside one: written as a property it sat next to the `"category"` range this would otherwise default to, and Vega reads the range, so a chart asking for `category20` got the ten-colour scheme. Otherwise, type from the field's type, the channel and the mark — a nominal field is a `band` under a rect-shaped mark and a `point` under anything else — with `zero`, `nice`, `padding`, `paddingInner`/`paddingOuter`, `bins`, `interpolate` and the range defaults, each gated by whether the scale type takes that property at all | — |
| Layout size | **Supported** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` (`bar`, `text-heatmap`, `tick`, `scale-overrides`, `step-for-position`) | `{"step": 50, "for": "position"}` hands the step to the **outer** band rather than to one mark inside it, so the step signal takes it outright and the offset divides whatever band it produced. A stated `scale.padding` likewise settles a band's two ends at once and passes through as it stands, the derived inner and outer paddings being for a scale that said nothing. A discrete position has no width of its own, so the chart is *derived* from a step per category and `width` becomes a signal: `bandspace(domain('x').length, 0.1, 0.05) * x_step`. A continuous position takes `config.view.continuousWidth`, 300. Only a **band** scale has a real inner padding there: a **point** scale counts as 1, because n points have n−1 steps between them, and its `padding` is the outer one — vega-scale builds `point()` as `pointish(band().paddingInner(1))` | — |
| Aggregation, binning, time units | **Supported** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` (`histogram`, `aggregate-bar`, `stacked-bar`, `binned-facet`, `binned-axis`, `aggregate-ops`, `gallery-rules`) | `aggregate` (including `count`, whose output field is the reserved `__count`), `bin` — as `true`, as parameters, or `"binned"` — and `timeUnit`. A column that arrives **already bucketed** — written either as `bin: "binned"` or as an object saying `binned: true`, both of which `isBinned` accepts, and reading only the string bins it a second time — takes the bucket's far edge from the secondary channel rather than from an `_end` column nobody computed, which is why that form requires an `x2`; the same edge is what the row is announced by and what groups the stack beside the start. Two layers aggregating one table are folded into **one** aggregate where they group by the same fields, upstream's `MergeAggregates`, and the union is a union of *measures*: the order follows each field's first appearance, which puts a `count` after every measure of a column. Field naming follows upstream's `vgField`, so `mean_b`, `bin_maxbins_10_v` and `sum_yield_end` are the names the transforms, the scale domains and the mark encodings all use — and the *signal* a `bin` publishes its chosen boundaries as is named through the view, so a binned field inside a facet is `child_bin_maxbins_10_v_bins` on both sides of the reference | — |
| Stacking | **Supported** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` (`bar`, `stacked-bar`, `area`, `faceted-density`) | A stacked path mark's **imputation** happens within `facetby.concat(stackby)`, so a gap in a trellis is filled from the cell's own rows rather than across the cells; and a path mark split into series reads the *cell's* partition rather than the whole table, which had been drawing every cell's series in every cell. Upstream's rules, which are asymmetric on purpose: with a measure on both axes the aggregated one stacks, and with neither aggregated a bar or an area falls back to its orientation. A stacked *path* mark imputes the gaps to zero first, or the band above a gap would step into it | — |
| Data | **Supported** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` (`layer-sources`, `concat-sources`) | `values`, `url` with a format, and `name`. **Every table the chart reads is a root**, in the order it was first asked for, because that order is the numbering — a layer or a plot with its own `data` gets a source of its own, without which its marks are drawn against the first view's rows, which is a wrong chart rather than a missing one — and a `lookup`'s joined table is a root too. Then `assembleRootData`'s last step: a dataset that derives from nothing and does nothing moves to the front, Vega having to hold a handed-over table before whatever joins against it. The dataset naming (`source_0`, `data_0`, …) follows upstream's flattening walk, because whether a view's transforms land on `source_0` or on a derived `data_0` decides what every scale domain and mark reads from. The implicit **parse** follows it too, and every case is a comparison that would otherwise be made between strings: a temporal field before a time scale sorts it, a `min`/`max` aggregate before it answers with the alphabetically smallest, and a path mark's own dimension before it draws a line through 1, 10, 2 in that order. Where several views share a dataset the parse is hoisted above the fork and *every* branch re-parented onto it — upstream's `MergeParse` — which is what puts a layer's own transforms after the shared parse rather than beside it. Three more of upstream's optimizers decide the same shape and so the same names: identical sibling branches fold into one (`MergeIdenticalNodes`), sibling aggregates grouping by the same fields fold into one (`MergeAggregates`), and a fork with a bare *output* branch in it collapses, because an output is a name and nothing else and everything at the fork belongs below it (`MergeOutputs`) — without which a chart whose second layer reads the raw rows numbers every dataset one too high | — |
| Time units | **Supported** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` (`timeunit-bar`, `temporal-units`, `timeunit-ordinal`) | The `timeunit` transform, a scale domain over the bucket's start *and* end, an axis labelled by the `timeUnitSpecifier(...)` Vega chooses at render time — beside a `formatType` saying to read that specifier as a *time* one, which the scale implies only where it is itself temporal, so a band of month names would otherwise be labelled with its milliseconds — and a `tickMinStep` of one bucket — expressed as the difference between two dates in a deliberately non-leap year, because no constant says how long a month is. A field is an instant when it is typed temporal **or** carries a time unit — `isFieldOrDatumDefForTimeFormat` — so a month bucketed onto an *ordinal* scale is parsed as a date, spoken as a date and labelled as a date, on its axis and in its legend alike; reading only the type announced the bucket's raw milliseconds. A `utc` unit carries `timezone: "utc"` on the transform, without which a midnight instant is bucketed against the viewer's calendar. The bucket's *end* joins the scale domain only for a mark that occupies the span, which upstream decides by whether the mark has a `timeUnitBandPosition`; and an axis over a cyclic unit — month, hours, day, quarter — asks for no tick count at all, since twelve months want twelve ticks rather than seven | — |

| A rect filling part of its bucket | **Supported** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` (`band-fraction`) | `{"width": {"band": 0.7}}` on the mark: both edges move in by half of what is left over, `(1 - band) / 2` and its complement. The interpolation happens in **data** space and the scale is applied to the result, which is not the same as interpolating two scaled positions once the scale is not linear | — |
| Titles written as `""` | **Supported** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` (`empty-title`) | One value with two opposite readings, both upstream's. `assembleTitle` writes `titleString ? {title} : {}`, so an axis titled `""` has **no** caption; the tooltip and the description read `fieldDef.title || defaultTitle(...)`, an `||`, so the same empty title falls through to the field's own name there | — |
| Numbers inside expressions | **Supported** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` (`band-fraction`, `binned-axis`) | Written the way `String(n)` writes one — the shortest text that reads back as the same double. `max(4.0, …)` and `max(4, …)` are the same instruction and a different string, and a specification is compared as text; the display canonicaliser is not the answer either, since it rounds, and `0.15000000000000002` is not `0.15` | — |

| `mark.clip` | **Partial** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` | A Vega mark property, not a Vega-Lite-only one, so it is emitted rather than stripped. | `mark.invalid` | **Supported** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` (`invalid-modes`, `invalid-shown`) | What a chart does with a value no scale can place. Upstream's default, `break-paths-show-path-domains`, is a macro for two behaviours — a path **breaks** at the gap and keeps the row, everything else **drops** the row — so the mode is normalised once per view and the mark's `defined` and the data flow's filter both read it: filtering a row the path was going to break at removes the break along with the row. `filter`, `break-paths-filter-domains` and `break-paths-show-domains` all compile. `defined` is asked once per **scaled** channel rather than once for x and y, so a line coloured by a continuous field breaks where that colour is missing, and a channel whose scale has a discrete domain is never invalid — a null is another category. Every other mode has already dealt with the row, so `show` is the only one that reaches the encoding | — |
| A field named through a path | **Supported** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` (`nested-fields`) | `record.high` is a path into a nested object in Vega-Lite and a column's *name* in Vega. The property is written even when it comes out empty, which is what upstream's node does. Beside it, the *derived* rule from `data/parse.ts`: a column a transform computed is never parsed, having the type its transform gave it and never having been seen by the loader | — |
| Trail | **Supported** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` (`trail`) | A line whose thickness follows the data, and two rules that are not the ones they look like. Its legend names two channels differently from every other mark's: colour goes on the `stroke` however the mark is filled, since the swatch is a short stroke, and `size` becomes `strokeWidth`. The **runtime** draws a `strokeWidth` legend at the symbol's size rather than the scale's stroke width, so the fixture states `legend: null` | — |
| An omitted channel `type` | **Supported** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` (`inferred-types`) | `defaultType`, which is read off the *definition* and not off the data — nothing has read a row when it runs. A latitude is quantitative and a shape, facet or `strokeDash` nominal whatever they carry; `order` is ordinal; a `sort` written as a **list** makes the field ordinal; a `timeUnit` makes it temporal; a `bin` or **any aggregate except `argmin`/`argmax`** makes it quantitative; a stated `scale.type` answers by category (numeric and discretizing → quantitative, time → temporal); and only then does it fall through to nominal, with a report. Falling straight through instead turns a summed measure into a category per distinct total, and draws a bar chart as a scatter of squares along a diagonal | — |
| Ranged positions (`x2`, `y2`) | **Supported** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` (`ranged-bar`, `rule-range`) | Both fields reach the scale domain and both names reach the axis title (`start, end`), and the secondary channel takes its type from the channel it bounds — `{"x2": {"field": "end"}}` is how every ranged mark is written | — |
| Sorting a discrete domain | **Supported** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` (`sorted-bar`, `sort-array`, `sort-by-channel`) | **`"-x"`** — a shorthand naming another *channel*, with the minus for descending, which is how most sorted bar charts are written: the categories are ordered by the aggregate of that channel's field, computed from the pre-aggregation table. Read as an unrecognised string it became the default alphabetical order, a chart sorted the wrong way rather than one that failed. Beside it, `"sort": "descending"`, a **written-out list** of values, a sort object over an aggregate of another field, or sorting by another channel's encoding. An aggregate sort reads the *pre-aggregation* table, which is named in the output for it, so the ordering is computed independently of the aggregation being drawn. A list is not a comparator Vega has: each row's *place* in it is computed as a column and the domain sorts on the smallest place a category carries, so a value the list never names falls past the end and lands last. A sort with no `op` takes `min`, or `sum` where the sort field is not one of the stack's own dimensions — sorting by a field each category has once means the smallest is the value, and sorting by a field the stack accumulates means asking which column is tallest | — |
| Transforms | **Partial** | `VegaLiteFixtureTest` (`transforms`, `reshape`, `impute-area`) | `calculate`, `filter`, `aggregate`, `joinaggregate`, `window`, `fold` and `flatten` — both of which always write their output names, filling in `key`/`value` and each field's own, since those names are what everything downstream groups and scales by — `pivot`, `sample`, `extent`, `quantile`, `density`, `regression` and `loess`, and `impute` — as a transform of its own, naming its field, key and grouping, or on a position channel, either way taking a `keyvals` written as a **sequence**, which becomes a `sequence(...)` signal — the *other* position is its key and a path mark's series fields are its grouping, so one series' gaps are filled from its own rows; a method other than `value` becomes a `window` beside it written back over the nulls,. A `filter` takes an expression or a **predicate object** — `equal`, `lt`/`lte`/`gt`/`gte`, `range`, `oneOf`, `valid`, over `and`/`or`/`not` — compiled to the expression upstream chose rather than an equivalent of it — including the two rules a **date** brings with it: a month or quarter written as a *number* is one-based to the reader and zero-based to `datetime()`, where one written as a *name* has already been resolved and must not be shifted again; and a **binned** time unit needs no bucketing on the way into the comparison, the column already holding the bucket — and a predicate also settles the *type* of the column it compares, which a CSV would not otherwise know. `lookup` joins a second table: its data stands *beside* the view's source rather than below it — a join reads a second table, it does not derive from the first — and is named in the same sequence.
